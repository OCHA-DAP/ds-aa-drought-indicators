Do temperature, rainfall and vegetation indicators predict drought impact?

A cross-country backtest for the ten countries where OCHA has an endorsed or in-development drought anticipatory-action framework: Afghanistan, Burkina Faso, Chad, El Salvador, Ethiopia, Guatemala, Honduras, Kenya, Mauritania and Niger. Every indicator behind our triggers, plus growing-season temperature, tested against real outcomes: national staple production (FAOSTAT, 2001 to 2024), CERF drought allocations dated to their deficit season, and EM-DAT drought events.

Bottom line. Our drought triggers key on rainfall and satellite vegetation. Across the ten countries, growing-season temperature is as good a predictor of staple-production shortfalls as the best vegetation index and better than any rainfall measure, and it is not what any framework uses. Pooled over 240 country-seasons, detrended temperature explains 13 percent of production variance out of sample; FAO's Agricultural Stress Index 14, mean VHI 13, ERA5 rainfall, SPI-3 and water balance 9 each, and ASAP's cumulative-FPAR biomass anomaly, the indicator two of our frameworks trigger on, 2 percent. Temperature and ASI together reach 19 percent and each keeps its own coefficient, so they carry different information. The strength is uneven: the signal is strong in the Sahel and Afghanistan, moderate in Chad and Kenya, and absent in Ethiopia and the Central American Dry Corridor, where national production does not follow the framework area's weather. Impact seasons are harder to rank than production because in several countries CERF has responded to drought in most years; where they are rare enough to test, temperature ranks them best in Burkina Faso, Niger, Chad and Afghanistan.

What was tested

  • Indicators, all from the JRC ASAP per-admin export (one consistent route, 2001 onward): temperature (ECMWF reanalysis, cropland), rainfall (CHIRPS), SPI-3, water satisfaction index (WSI), cumulative-FPAR anomaly (zFPARc, cropland and rangeland). Plus ERA5 monthly rainfall from the team database, and FAO GIEWS ASIS: the Agricultural Stress Index (share of cropland under stress in the season) and mean VHI, at admin 1. Temperature is detrended per unit (Theil-Sen, 1991 to 2025), biomass per unit (2001 to 2025), so that "hot" means hotter than that year would be expected to be.
  • Season and area. Each country's main growing season (dekads in data/config/countries.json); indicators averaged over the framework area and over the national mean of ASAP units. Biomass, WSI and SPI-3 are averaged over the last 60 percent of the season, where the trigger windows sit.
  • Targets. Continuous: national production of the country's staples (millet and sorghum in the Sahel, wheat in Afghanistan, maize and beans in Central America, maize in Kenya, all cereals in Ethiopia), as the percentage departure from a 2001 to 2024 linear trend. Binary: growing seasons with a CERF drought allocation dated to that season by the CERF supplement's rainfall-deficit period, or an EM-DAT drought event whose start follows that season. Undated chronic allocations are a sensitivity only.
  • Statistics. Ordinary least squares on standardised predictors with leave-one-out R² (the honest number with 24 seasons), AUC for the binary target, and a pooled regression with every series standardised within its country so that only within-country year-to-year variation is compared.
